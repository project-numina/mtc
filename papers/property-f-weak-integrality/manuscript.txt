        PROPERTY F IMPLIES WEAK INTEGRALITY
    FOR PSEUDO-UNITARY BRAIDED FUSION CATEGORIES

                Abstract. We give a proof by contradiction that every braided
                fusion category with property F and a pseudo-unitary Galois
                conjugate is weakly integral. The proof combines a trace in-
                equality for finite braid group images with Galois symmetry
                in the Drinfeld centre. A non-pseudo-unitary conjugate would
                produce a Galois image of the unit whose conjugated dimen-
                sion lies strictly between zero and one. The trace inequality
                forces the absolute value of this dimension to equal the object’s
                Frobenius–Perron dimension, which is at least one.

   Let C be a braided fusion category over C. For an object X and n ≥ 2, write GX,n
for the image of

The category has property F if every GX,n is finite, and is weakly integral if
FPdim(C) ∈ Z.
Conjecture (Naidu–Rowell [NR]). A braided fusion category has property F if and
only if it is weakly integral.
   Thus the conjecture relates finiteness of all braid group images to a single arithmetic
condition on the fusion category. We prove the implication from property F to weak
integrality under a pseudo-unitarity hypothesis. Recall that C is pseudo-unitary if
dim(C) = FPdim(C).
Theorem 1.1. Let C be a braided fusion category with a pseudo-unitary Galois
conjugate. If C has property F, then C is weakly integral.
   The proof takes place in the Drinfeld centre of a pseudo-unitary Galois conjugate
of C. We show that every further Galois conjugate remains pseudo-unitary; its global
dimension is then fixed by Galois conjugation and hence is an integer. If a conjugate
were not pseudo-unitary, Galois symmetry in the centre would produce a simple
object Y whose dimension in a conjugate category lies strictly between zero and one.
Finite braid images force the absolute value of this dimension to equal FPdim(Y ),
which is at least one.
   The main step is the trace argument in Section 3. It first bounds the categorical
trace of every unitary in a braid algebra. A particular three-strand braid attains this
bound; the equality case then gives the required identity of dimensions. Section 4
places the relevant Galois images of the unit in a subcategory of the centre with
property F and completes the contradiction. It then derives a criterion for a single
object to have infinite braid images.

                                  2. Preliminaries
   All fusion categories are over C, and we suppress associativity and unit constraints.
We write 1 for the tensor unit and NZW = dim Hom(W, Z) for the multiplicity of a
simple object W in an object Z.
2.1. Transfer of property F. Property F passes to direct summands and fusion
subcategories. Indeed, if V is a summand of X, naturality of the braiding makes
V ⊗n invariant under the braid action on X ⊗n , and restriction gives a quotient of
GX,n . It also passes along a dominant braided tensor functor: the braid image on
the image of an object is a quotient of its original braid image, and every object of
the target is a summand of such an image.
   If C has property F, so do C rev and C ⊠ C rev . Reversing the braiding inverts
the braid generators. On X ⊠ Y , the braid image is a quotient of a subgroup of
GX,n × GY,n , and every object of the product category is a summand of some X ⊠ Y .
   Finally, property F and Frobenius–Perron dimensions are invariant under Galois
conjugation. A number-field model for the braided categorical data exists by [ENO,
2.30, 2.33]. Conjugation preserves all relations among braid matrices and preserves
the fusion rules. We regard Galois automorphisms as automorphisms of Q over Q.

a character of the fusion ring, as is each of its Galois conjugates. Thus σ(dX ) is a
fusion-matrix eigenvalue, and Perron–Frobenius theory gives

                    4. The main theorem and consequences
   To apply Lemma 3.2, we place the Galois images of the unit in a modular category
where their braid images are finite. The required category is the Drinfeld centre; the
relevant objects lie in a subcategory generated by the canonical images of C and C rev .
   For a fusion subcategory A of a braided category, write A′ for its centralizer. Let

Lemma 4.1. Let C be a pseudo-unitary braided fusion category with property F.
Then M = Z(C) is pseudo-unitary and modular, and B = C+ ∨ C− has property F
and contains τb(1) for every Galois automorphism τ .
Proof. The centre is modular. Its global and Frobenius–Perron dimensions are
dim(C)2 and FPdim(C)2 , respectively, so it is pseudo-unitary [EGNO, 7.16.6, 8.20.14,
9.3.4]. The canonical images are mutual centralizers inside M: (V, γ) centralizes C+
precisely when γX = c−1X,V for every X, and similarly for C− . Consequently,

                                 B ′ = C+ ∩ C− = ι+ (C ′ ).
This category is symmetric, hence integral [DGNO, 2.46]. For Y = τb(1) and every
simple X ∈ B ′ , we therefore have dX = FPdim(X) ∈ Z, and
                                SXY = dY τ (dX ) = dY dX .
The S-matrix criterion for centralizers and the double-centralizer theorem give
Y ∈ B ′′ = B [EGNO, 8.20.5, 8.21.1]. Finally, the two canonical images centralize
each other, so X ⊠Y 7→ ι+ (X)⊗ι− (Y ) defines a braided functor C ⊠C rev → B [EGNO,
8.6.1]. It is dominant because these images generate B. The transfer properties in
Section 2.1 therefore give property F for B.                                     □

   The theorem also has an objectwise consequence. To apply it to the fusion
subcategory ⟨X⟩ generated by an object X, we must establish property F for every
object of that subcategory. The following lemma shows that finiteness of all GX,n
suffices.
Lemma 4.2 (Closure). Let X be an object of a braided fusion category. If GX,n is
finite for every n, then ⟨X⟩ has property F.
Proof. The case X = 0 is immediate. Otherwise, summands of finite sums of positive
powers of X form a nonzero full abelian subcategory closed under tensor products
and subquotients. By [EGNO, 4.11.4], it contains the unit and is rigid, so it equals
⟨X⟩. Thus every object of ⟨X⟩ is a summand of some Z = i X ⊗ki , with ki ≥ 1. For
                                                             L

each n ≥ 2, the pure braid group Pn preserves each summand X ⊗ki1 ⊗ · · · ⊗ X ⊗kin of
Z ⊗n . By cabling, its action there has image in the finite group GX,ki1 +···+kin . Thus
its image on Z ⊗n is finite, being contained in a finite product of finite groups. Since
[Bn : Pn ] = n!, the full braid image is finite as well [ERW, 2.1(ii)]. Naturality passes
finiteness to every summand of Z, as in Section 2.1.                                   □
  Consequently, the dimension of a particular simple object can force its own braid
images to be infinite.
Corollary 4.3. Let X be a simple object of a braided fusion category, and suppose
that ⟨X⟩ has a pseudo-unitary Galois conjugate. If FPdim(X)2 ∈  / Z, then GX,n is
infinite for every sufficiently large n.
Proof. If every GX,n were finite, Lemma 4.2 and Theorem 1.1 would make ⟨X⟩
weakly integral. Then FPdim(X)2 ∈ Z by [ENO, 8.27], a contradiction. Hence
GX,n0 is infinite for some n0 . Tensoring with a nonzero object is faithful, so tensoring
with identity strands embeds GX,n0 in GX,n for every n ≥ n0 .                          □
   In particular, the conclusion holds in every unitary braided fusion category, since its
fusion subcategories are unitary and hence pseudo-unitary. The simplicity hypothesis
is essential: direct sums in a weakly integral category need not have integral squared
Frobenius–Perron dimension.
